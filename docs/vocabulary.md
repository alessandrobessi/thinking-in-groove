# Conceptual Vocabulary (Legacy)

> Superseded by [`vocabulary-v2.md`](vocabulary-v2.md). This file is retained only as a record of the original taxonomy and must not govern new prose or examples.

Thinking in Groove does not teach bass through scales or arpeggios. It
teaches through three questions, and each question has its own
vocabulary:

- **Role** — what job is this bass note/line doing?
- **Motion** — how does it move the harmony?
- **Groove** — how does it create rhythmic feel?

A fourth category, **Integration**, covers terms for how Role, Motion,
and Groove combine into a finished bass line.

Every term below becomes exactly one chapter — see
[`chapter-map.md`](chapter-map.md) for the numbered order.

## A. Role — What Job Is the Bass Doing?

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Anchor** | A bass note that grounds the harmony, establishing stability at the key center with minimal movement. |
| **Definer** | A bass note chosen specifically to define chord quality (e.g. the 3rd or 7th) rather than just stating the root. |
| **Connector** | A note whose job is to link two harmonic areas, functioning as directional passing motion. |
| **Driver** | A bass line whose primary job is rhythmic propulsion — momentum outweighs harmonic duty. |
| **Colorist** | A bass note chosen for harmonic color (e.g. a 9th or 13th in the bass) rather than functional duty. |
| **Shadow** | A bass line that doubles another instrument's line (unison/octave) to reinforce a melodic idea. |
| **Voice-Leader** | A bass line whose note choices are dictated by smooth motion between chord tones rather than root movement. |
| **Commentator** | A bass line that converses rhythmically/melodically with other instruments, independent of strict harmonic duty. |
| **Role Shift** | The moment within a phrase where a bass line changes its primary Role (e.g. Anchor → Connector). |

## B. Harmonic Motion — How the Bass Moves the Music

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Root Motion** | The interval and direction between successive chord roots as stated by the bass. |
| **Pedal** | Sustaining or repeating one bass note underneath changing harmony above it. |
| **Passing Motion** | Stepwise bass movement connecting two harmonic points, implying intermediate harmony. |
| **Approach Note** | A bass note (chromatic or diatonic) that leads into a target note from a half- or whole-step away. |
| **Substituted Root** | Using a note other than the expected root to imply a different underlying chord. |
| **Deceptive Motion** | Bass motion that sets up an expected resolution, then diverts to a different destination. |
| **Cadential Motion** | Bass movement that signals the end of a phrase through tension and release. |
| **Harmonic Rhythm** | The rate at which the bass line itself implies chord change, independent of what other instruments do. |
| **Motion Profile** | The overall harmonic shape a bass line traces across a phrase (static, ascending, cyclical, wandering). |

## C. Groove — How the Bass Creates Feel

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Pocket** | The precise rhythmic placement of a note relative to the underlying pulse (ahead, dead-center, behind). |
| **Syncopation Point** | A specific subdivision where the bass deliberately avoids the strong beat to create rhythmic tension. |
| **Space** | Intentional silence used as a rhythmic/harmonic device — rest as content, not absence of content. |
| **Repetition Cell** | A short rhythmic-melodic figure repeated to establish a groove's identity (a "riff atom"). |
| **Variation Layer** | A controlled alteration of a repetition cell (displacement, substitution, ornament) that keeps a groove alive without losing its identity. |
| **Density** | The number of note-events per beat/measure, controlling perceived energy. |
| **Push / Lay-back** | Deliberate rhythmic anticipation (push) or delay (lay-back) relative to the beat. |
| **Groove Signature** | The combination of pocket, density, and repetition cell that makes a bass line recognizable as a style. |

## D. Integration — Combining Role, Motion, and Groove

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Layer Stack** | The simultaneous combination of a Role choice, a Motion choice, and a Groove choice within one bass line. |
| **Groove Contract** | The implicit rhythmic promise established in a piece's opening measures, which a listener expects honored — or knowingly broken. |

*Note: "Reharmonization Vector" was considered as a standalone term but
folded into Substituted Root and Motion Profile to avoid redundancy
(Book Architect acceptance criterion in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md)).*
