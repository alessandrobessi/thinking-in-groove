# Accompaniment Style

The right hand is evidence, not decoration. It must make the bass concept audible by supplying at least one necessary reference: harmonic identity, meter, subdivision, phrase boundary, call and response, contrary motion, registral contrast, or controlled density.

## Defaults

- Prefer compact three- or four-note voicings between C3 and C6.
- Omit the root above when doubling would blur the point being tested.
- Keep rhythm simple in a microscope and musical in a study.
- Avoid continuous pianistic figuration unless interaction with that figuration is the lesson.
- Leave enough low-register space for the bass to remain legible.
- Make a study satisfying at its written tempo and at the slow practice tempo.

## Controlled comparisons

Copy the right-hand voice exactly between variants. If a chord symbol must change to name the resulting inversion, the sounded upper notes still remain identical. Do not “improve” the accompaniment between A and B.

## Review questions

1. What reference does the right hand provide?
2. Would removing it make the concept ambiguous?
3. Does it compete with the bass in register, rhythm, or density?
4. Does the full texture sound intentional without the annotation?
