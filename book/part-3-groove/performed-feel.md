# Performed Feel

*Chapter 27 — Part III, Groove: How Is Motion Organized in Time? Closing chapter: names notation's limits; introduces no new device.*

## The Question

Once you've notated every rhythmic decision as precisely as this Part allows, what's still missing from the page?

## The Mental Model

**Performed Feel** is the recognition that everything this Part has covered — Subdivision, Attack Placement, Duration, Space, Syncopation, Anticipation, Repeated Cells, Variation Without Collapse, Phrase Rhythm — describes what notation can show, and notation has a limit. It cannot fully encode touch, physical attack, drummer interaction, a stable ensemble Pocket, or an exact human microtiming profile. Playback demonstrates the written claim; it is not proof of a human feel.

This isn't a new rhythmic device to learn. It's the honest boundary around everything else in this Part: two performers can read the identical notation and produce audibly different pockets, and the page will never show you which one is "correct," because correctness at that level isn't something notation was ever built to specify.

Both examples show the identical notation. Nothing on the page distinguishes them — that absence of difference is the entire point.

## Listen

Play **Full** on both and listen closely for a difference. There isn't one — this book's playback synthesizes notation literally, at a fixed mechanical timing, precisely because that's all notation alone can specify. A live performer reading either panel could still choose to sit behind the beat, push ahead of it, or lock dead center, and every one of those choices would be equally consistent with the page.

## See

Compare the two raw ABC sources directly if you view the page source: they're identical except for the title. That's not an oversight — it's the whole demonstration. If you were expecting the notation to look different once you knew "B is felt differently," that expectation is exactly the common error this chapter exists to correct.

:::

Play this exercise three times without changing a single written note: once trying to sit slightly ahead of a mental click, once slightly behind, once dead center. Record all three if you can. The notation you're reading from never changes; what you're practicing is a skill this chapter's own vocabulary has no written symbol for.

## Vary

Ask another musician to play "Play It Three Ways" without telling them which of the three feels to use. Can you identify which one they chose purely by ear? What does your answer suggest about how much of "feel" really is audible, even though none of it is written?
