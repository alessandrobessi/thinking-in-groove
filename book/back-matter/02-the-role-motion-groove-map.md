# The Role / Motion / Groove Map

*Back matter — Integrated Study and Appendices.*

A complete quick-reference chart of every vocabulary term in this book, grouped by category, each with its one-line definition (copied verbatim from `docs/vocabulary.md`) and chapter number. Use this page the way you'd use a harmonic cheat-sheet: not to learn a term for the first time, but to look one up fast while transcribing a line you're studying or composing one of your own.

## How to use this page

While transcribing: for each bass note or short phrase you're studying, ask the three questions this book keeps returning to — what job is it doing (Role), how is it moving the harmony (Motion), how does it sit in time (Groove) — and use the tables below to find the exact term that names your answer. If nothing in a table quite fits, the closest term is usually good enough; this vocabulary is meant to compress judgment, not to replace it.

While composing: work top-down, the way Chapter 29 demonstrates — pick a Motion Profile and a Groove Signature for the whole phrase first, then assign a Role to each structurally important note, then fill in the Motion and Groove details in between. These tables are ordered to support that: skim Harmonic Motion and Groove first for the phrase-level terms (Motion Profile, Groove Signature, Groove Contract), then use Role for the note-level decisions.

## A. Role — What Job Is the Bass Doing? (Chapters 1–9)

| Chapter | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anchor** | A bass note that grounds the harmony, establishing stability at the key center with minimal movement. |
| 2 | **Definer** | A bass note chosen specifically to define chord quality (e.g. the 3rd or 7th) rather than just stating the root. |
| 3 | **Connector** | A note whose job is to link two harmonic areas, functioning as directional passing motion. |
| 4 | **Driver** | A bass line whose primary job is rhythmic propulsion — momentum outweighs harmonic duty. |
| 5 | **Colorist** | A bass note chosen for harmonic color (e.g. a 9th or 13th in the bass) rather than functional duty. |
| 6 | **Shadow** | A bass line that doubles another instrument's line (unison/octave) to reinforce a melodic idea. |
| 7 | **Voice-Leader** | A bass line whose note choices are dictated by smooth motion between chord tones rather than root movement. |
| 8 | **Commentator** | A bass line that converses rhythmically/melodically with other instruments, independent of strict harmonic duty. |
| 9 | **Role Shift** | The moment within a phrase where a bass line changes its primary Role (e.g. Anchor → Connector). |

## B. Harmonic Motion — How the Bass Moves the Music (Chapters 10–18)

| Chapter | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Root Motion** | The interval and direction between successive chord roots as stated by the bass. |
| 11 | **Pedal** | Sustaining or repeating one bass note underneath changing harmony above it. |
| 12 | **Passing Motion** | Stepwise bass movement connecting two harmonic points, implying intermediate harmony. |
| 13 | **Approach Note** | A bass note (chromatic or diatonic) that leads into a target note from a half- or whole-step away. |
| 14 | **Substituted Root** | Using a note other than the expected root to imply a different underlying chord. |
| 15 | **Deceptive Motion** | Bass motion that sets up an expected resolution, then diverts to a different destination. |
| 16 | **Cadential Motion** | Bass movement that signals the end of a phrase through tension and release. |
| 17 | **Harmonic Rhythm** | The rate at which the bass line itself implies chord change, independent of what other instruments do. |
| 18 | **Motion Profile** | The overall harmonic shape a bass line traces across a phrase (static, ascending, cyclical, wandering). |

## C. Groove — How the Bass Creates Feel (Chapters 19–26)

| Chapter | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | **Pocket** | The precise rhythmic placement of a note relative to the underlying pulse (ahead, dead-center, behind). |
| 20 | **Syncopation Point** | A specific subdivision where the bass deliberately avoids the strong beat to create rhythmic tension. |
| 21 | **Space** | Intentional silence used as a rhythmic/harmonic device — rest as content, not absence of content. |
| 22 | **Repetition Cell** | A short rhythmic-melodic figure repeated to establish a groove's identity (a "riff atom"). |
| 23 | **Variation Layer** | A controlled alteration of a repetition cell (displacement, substitution, ornament) that keeps a groove alive without losing its identity. |
| 24 | **Density** | The number of note-events per beat/measure, controlling perceived energy. |
| 25 | **Push / Lay-back** | Deliberate rhythmic anticipation (push) or delay (lay-back) relative to the beat. |
| 26 | **Groove Signature** | The combination of pocket, density, and repetition cell that makes a bass line recognizable as a style. |

## D. Integration — Combining Role, Motion, and Groove (Chapters 27–29)

| Chapter | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | **Layer Stack** | The simultaneous combination of a Role choice, a Motion choice, and a Groove choice within one bass line. |
| 28 | **Groove Contract** | The implicit rhythmic promise established in a piece's opening measures, which a listener expects honored — or knowingly broken. |
| 29 | *(capstone — no new term)* | A worked, start-to-finish design of one original bass line, recombining the full vocabulary above. |

## Quick lookup by question

If you're not sure which category your question falls into, start here:

- "What is this note *for*?" → **Role** (A)
- "Where is this note *going*, harmonically?" → **Harmonic Motion** (B)
- "*When*, exactly, does this note land?" → **Groove** (C)
- "Why do these three answers, taken together, work — or not?" → **Integration** (D)
