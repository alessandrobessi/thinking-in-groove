# The Ground

*Chapter 1 — Part I, Role: What Is the Bass Responsible For?*

## The Question

Why does a chord feel physically settled when its root appears below it?

## The Mental Model

The lowest note is the floor from which the ear measures the rest of a sonority. When the chord root occupies that floor, chord identity and bass weight agree. The result usually feels direct and settled.

The **Ground** is the bass acting as harmonic foundation by placing the root beneath the harmony. Grounding is not the same as sustaining. A long third is sustained but does not establish root position; a short root attack can ground a chord even after it releases.

## Listen

Hear **Harmony only** first: all four versions share the same upper notes. Then compare **Full** playback. A has both low weight and root identity. B preserves quality without a floor. C and D have low weight, but the floor reorganizes the chord.

## See

The bass annotation names a responsibility, not a duration. In A, each half-note C re-establishes the root. In C and D, equally long notes perform different harmonic jobs. Length alone cannot make a note the Ground.

## Play

Keep each right-hand chord identical and play the roots below it. Release the bass on beat 3 while the harmony continues; notice whether the short root attack still established the bar.

## Vary

Preserve every pitch and attack. Shorten each bass root from a half note to a quarter note. Does grounding weaken, or does only the texture become lighter?

## Reflection

In the study, which root attacks establish the harmony immediately, and which later bass notes merely decorate a floor the ear already accepted?
